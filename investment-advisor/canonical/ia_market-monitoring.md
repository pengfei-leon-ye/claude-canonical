# Market Monitoring Method

> **Status:** transformed chat-project replica; not SOT.
> **Authoritative source(s):** `2_topics/investment-advising/market-monitoring.md`; `3_projects/investment-advisor/canonical/investor-strategy.md`.
> **Conversation boundary:** This document governs in-conversation review of available market evidence. It produces analysis and possible recommendations only; it does not claim that any trade occurred.

## 1. Purpose and Honest Scope

Market monitoring is a gray-rhino sensor. It looks for knowable, material, often-underweighted risks and opportunities that could affect the active portfolio doctrine.

It does not predict genuinely unforecastable shocks. Its useful contribution is:

- earlier recognition of material evidence;
- clearer connection between an event and the portfolio;
- disciplined routing to the right thesis, allocation, or risk question;
- explicit conditions for escalation, de-escalation, or closure.

A calm assessment with no recommended change is valid.

## 2. Evidence Discipline

For every material finding:

- identify the underlying source and its authority;
- state the as-of date;
- separate what happened from why it may matter;
- distinguish confirmed fact, reported claim, and inference;
- surface material uncertainty or conflicting evidence;
- do not infer current holdings or exposures that the investor has not supplied.

Current regulatory, policy, macro, valuation, quote, fundamental, and news claims require current evidence.

## 3. Watch Categories

Weight attention by the investor's actual exposures, but do not let holdings narrow the portfolio-level risk scan. A risk to the China-overweight thesis remains relevant even when it maps to no single holding.

| Category | What to examine | Main portfolio question |
|---|---|---|
| Policy and regulation | Fiscal and monetary stance, sector policy, capital-market access, listing economics, and rules affecting eligible instruments. | Does the evidence change a holding thesis, eligibility, or a China-overweight flip condition? |
| Geopolitics and strategic regime | Trade restrictions, sanctions, conflict risk, technology access, supply-chain pressure, and capital-flow constraints. | Does the evidence impair a theme, market access, or the expected China growth premium? |
| Macro and liquidity | Growth, inflation, credit, rates, currency, liquidity, and material fund-flow evidence. | Does the evidence alter valuation context, sleeve risk, or strategic confidence? |
| Market structure and portfolio exposure | Sector prosperity, valuation extremes, earnings warnings, industry-chain prices, and thesis-relevant company evidence. | Does a holding thesis, concentration limit, or target zone require review? |
| Global cross-asset context | External rates, broad risk aversion, commodities, currency, gold, and relevant non-China stress. | Does the Hedge sleeve or total risk posture need reconsideration? |
| Open thesis threads | Unresolved flip conditions, prior watch items, and evidence gaps. | What would confirm, escalate, weaken, or close the thread? |

## 4. Severity Tiers

| Tier | Meaning | In-conversation response |
|---|---|---|
| `Tier 1` / Note | Relevant but not action-changing. | Record the fact and why it remains non-actionable. |
| `Tier 2` / Watch | Could affect a thesis, allocation, or risk limit if it develops. | Explain the live uncertainty and the condition that would escalate or close it. |
| `Tier 3` / Act-candidate | Materially threatens a thesis, approaches or breaches a limit, or supports a declared flip condition. | Reassess the relevant doctrine immediately and state a strategy-derived recommendation if the evidence is sufficient. |

Ambiguous but potentially high-impact evidence should receive more scrutiny than certainty alone would imply. This is an invitation to examine the issue, not permission to overstate the evidence.

## 5. Finding Analysis

For each material finding, provide:

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Category | One watch category or named open thread. |
| What happened | Concise factual description. |
| Source and as-of | Underlying source, authority, and date. |
| Why it matters | Connection to a holding thesis, sleeve, risk limit, hedge, or portfolio flip condition. |
| Severity | `Tier 1`, `Tier 2`, or `Tier 3`, with rationale. |
| Rival interpretation | Strongest plausible reason the finding may matter less or differently. |
| Escalation condition | Evidence that would raise severity. |
| Closure condition | Evidence that would resolve or retire the concern. |
| Strategy route | Thesis review, rebalance analysis, hedge review, target-zone reconsideration, or no action. |

Severity must follow the portfolio impact, not headline intensity.

## 6. Connection to Strategy

| Finding type | Strategy question |
|---|---|
| Thesis-threatening company or sector evidence | Is the thesis still `Intact`, now `Watch`, or `Broken`? |
| Allocation drift or risk-limit pressure | Has a sleeve band, position cap, or Risk ceiling been approached or breached? |
| China-systemic deterioration | Has a China-overweight flip condition gained durable support? |
| Global risk stress | Does the Hedge sleeve still provide the intended insurance, and is its target adequate? |
| Valuation extreme | Does valuation justify considering an action before any technical placement analysis? |
| Price-zone readiness | Are thesis and valuation already sufficient, and has the act-if-reached condition become relevant? |
| Evidence uncertainty | What exact current input is needed before a conclusion is defensible? |

Monitoring evidence never justifies a trade by itself. It must connect to an existing doctrine, limit, thesis judgment, or valuation case.

## 7. Portfolio-Specific Systemic Watch

The following domains directly test the China-overweight thesis:

- structural debt and property-related stress;
- banking-system transmission;
- productivity and demographic trajectory;
- currency and capital-account pressure;
- policy toward private capital formation;
- accessibility of the investor's eligible market channels;
- economics of domestic growth-market listings;
- decoupling severe enough to impair the expected growth premium.

These domains stay relevant independently of current sector holdings.

## 8. Conversation Output

When the investor asks for a market-risk review, answer in this order:

1. **Overall posture:** calm, watch, or act-candidate.
2. **Most important finding:** what changed and why it matters.
3. **Material findings:** grouped by category with evidence and severity.
4. **Portfolio implications:** thesis, sleeve, concentration, hedge, or valuation effect.
5. **Recommended response:** action, focused review, evidence request, or no action.
6. **Open threads:** escalation and closure conditions.

Do not pad a quiet evidence set with low-value headlines. Do not turn every `Tier 2` concern into a transaction recommendation.

## 9. Hard Boundaries

- No prediction of unforecastable shocks.
- No unsupported market or regulatory claim.
- No severity rating without a portfolio-impact explanation.
- No trade conclusion without a strategy route and adequate evidence.
- No inference that a proposed response has already occurred.
- No use of technical price movement as a substitute for a thesis or valuation judgment.
