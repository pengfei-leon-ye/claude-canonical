# Investor Strategy Doctrine

> **Status:** transformed chat-project replica; not SOT.
> **Authoritative source(s):** `3_projects/investment-advisor/canonical/investor-strategy.md`; `2_topics/investment-advising/methodology.md`.
> **Conversation boundary:** This document supplies investor-specific doctrine for discussion and recommendation. A recommendation is not an investor-confirmed transaction and never changes the stated portfolio facts.

## 1. Strategy Identity

This strategy belongs to one investor with a concentrated, China-overweight equity portfolio. It is a deliberate active posture rather than a globally diversified default. Its aim is disciplined long-term ownership, bounded concentration, and deliberate use of valuation-led opportunities.

The portfolio has three sleeves:

| Sleeve | Target | Primary job |
|---|---:|---|
| Stable / core | 45% | Preservation, broad China exposure, lower monitoring burden, and steady compounding. |
| Risk / satellite | 45% | Concentrated future-leader positions, chip accumulation, and cost-basis improvement while theses remain intact. |
| Hedge | 10% | Tail insurance against China-systematic shock; not a return source or generic opportunity reserve. |

In an elevated-risk regime, the Hedge sleeve may rise toward 15%, with Stable and Risk reduced symmetrically toward 42.5% each. The Risk sleeve has a hard ceiling of 65% of total portfolio value regardless of ordinary rebalance bands.

## 2. China-Overweight Thesis

The portfolio deliberately concentrates on China A-share markets and Hong Kong-listed or Stock-Connect-accessible China exposure. The thesis is that these markets can outperform over a 5–10 year horizon. This is a falsifiable conviction, not an article of faith.

Hold the thesis through ordinary cyclical drawdowns unless one or more flip conditions are supported by durable evidence:

1. **Structural breakdown:** long-run productivity, debt, demographic, or related growth drivers deteriorate in a sustained way rather than through a normal cycle.
2. **Private-capital hostility:** policy becomes structurally hostile to private capital formation or materially impairs the investor's accessible investment channels.
3. **Growth-premium decoupling:** geopolitical or economic decoupling becomes severe enough to remove the growth premium on which the thesis depends.

If a flip condition is met, the disciplined response is to reduce the China overweight and widen the Hedge sleeve. The response is a controlled change in exposure, not an emotional abandonment of the overall method.

Every discussion of this thesis must:

- test evidence both for and against it;
- distinguish cyclical weakness from structural deterioration;
- state the strongest live rival interpretation;
- name the evidence that would confirm or overturn the current judgment.

## 3. Risk-Sleeve Objective: Chip Thinking

The Risk sleeve is judged by ownership accumulated and cost basis improved on target holdings, not by realized cash profit alone. A successful cycle either preserves the desired ownership at a lower cost basis or converts volatility into more ownership for the same capital.

Chip thinking is valid only while the fundamental thesis remains intact. Once a thesis is `Broken`, accumulating more shares is no longer a valid objective. The disciplined route is reduction, exit, or renewed analysis.

## 4. Selection Doctrine

Selection follows:

```text
macro direction -> sub-industry node -> candidate universe -> finalist basket
```

### 4.1 Macro Direction

Start with long-horizon China policy and strategic direction, including:

- industrial upgrading;
- technology self-reliance and domestic substitution;
- energy and supply-chain security;
- digital economy and AI;
- green and low-carbon transition;
- geopolitical forces that change market access, supply chains, or capital allocation.

Current policy claims must be supported by current evidence. A durable theme does not make every company in that theme investable.

### 4.2 Sub-Industry Node

Prefer sub-industries where:

- penetration remains early-to-mid rather than mature;
- competitive structure is forming but not permanently closed;
- a moat-formation mechanism can be named and tested;
- policy direction and commercial demand reinforce rather than substitute for one another;
- the exposure does not merely duplicate another selected theme.

Avoid red-ocean commodity structures, stories that depend only on policy language, and settled industries where the claimed future-leader upside is already exhausted.

### 4.3 Company or Instrument

Compare candidates on:

- research and development intensity and pipeline credibility;
- market-share trajectory rather than current size alone;
- evidence that a moat is forming;
- management and governance quality;
- a valuation ceiling that prevents growth-at-any-price reasoning.

An existing holding receives no preference merely because it is already owned. Every held name must re-earn its place against the same criteria as a new candidate.

For an unsettled future-leader theme, prefer a basket of 2–3 differentiated names rather than a single winner prediction. The Risk sleeve normally contains 8–12 names across 3–5 themes. More than roughly 15 names is presumed to exceed monitoring capacity unless a specific reason justifies the expansion.

The Stable sleeve defaults to broad-base and dividend or low-volatility exchange-traded exposure rather than individual-stock concentration.

## 5. Trading Doctrine

Valuation plus thesis determines whether an action is justified. Technical analysis only refines the price band and timing condition after the action is justified.

The normal self-funding cycle is:

1. Establish or add when valuation is attractive and the thesis is intact.
2. Trim a tranche when valuation is expensive and the thesis remains intact.
3. Preserve the proceeds for a later valuation-supported add or an approved reallocation.
4. Judge the complete cycle by ownership and cost-basis improvement, not by whether one isolated decision looked clever.

Routine trims and adds use approximately one-third tranches. All-in or all-out behavior is excluded except for a `Broken` thesis or another explicitly reasoned full-exit case.

Trim intensity varies by business quality:

- **High-conviction compounder in a primary uptrend:** low trim intensity; ordinary secondary corrections are not a reason to sell it away.
- **Cyclical or mean-reverting holding:** higher trim intensity may be appropriate when valuation and thesis support the cycle.

Technical weakness cannot create a sell thesis, and technical strength cannot create a buy thesis. It also cannot veto an action already required by a broken thesis or binding concentration limit.

## 6. Sizing and Rebalancing Controls

| Control | Committed value |
|---|---|
| Single Risk position cap | At most approximately 15% of the Risk sleeve and approximately 8% of the total portfolio. |
| Risk-sleeve ceiling | At most 65% of the total portfolio. |
| Risk-position floor | Approximately 2–3% of the Risk sleeve unless a smaller position clearly earns its monitoring cost. |
| Sleeve rebalance band | Approximately ±7–10 percentage points, with room for asymmetry that avoids trimming a winning thesis too early. |
| Routine tranche | Approximately one-third of the intended move. |
| Hedge target | Normally 10%; may rise toward 15% under an elevated-risk judgment. |

Inter-sleeve rebalancing and intra-sleeve cycling are different decisions:

- inter-sleeve rebalancing restores the Stable / Risk / Hedge architecture when a band or ceiling is breached;
- intra-sleeve cycling changes an individual position only when thesis, valuation, sizing, and price-zone conditions agree.

The Hedge sleeve is insurance. Using it as generic cash merely because another asset appears attractive violates its purpose.

## 7. Human-Capital Concentration

Financial exposure must underweight the employer and career sector in which the investor's human capital is already concentrated.

- Employer stock stays well below the normal single-position cap and is biased toward minimal, regardless of thesis quality.
- No new satellite theme is added in the investor's career sector.
- Proceeds from employer-stock reduction go toward less-correlated sleeves or themes.
- Expected equity vesting is considered when judging future correlated exposure.

Human-capital context cannot be inferred from brokerage holdings. If it has not been supplied or is stale, the advisor must ask before making a concentration-sensitive recommendation.

## 8. Venue Doctrine

A-share and Hong Kong-listed exposure are not interchangeable, but their mixture does not diversify away the country-level China thesis.

- In the Stable sleeve, venue may be tilted where doing so does not undermine the sleeve's role.
- In the Risk sleeve, venue follows the best future-leader candidate and is an output of selection rather than a pre-filter.
- If a hard venue constraint is required, an H-share ceiling may bound foreign-flow or access risk; an H-share floor is rejected when it would force inferior candidates.
- Venue is a graded risk input, not a fixed sub-allocation.

Any venue conclusion must be supported by current evidence about liquidity, valuation, access, and the specific risk being discussed.

## 9. Review Lenses

Use separate lenses so short-term price movement does not dominate long-term judgment:

| Lens | Main question |
|---|---|
| Light valuation and allocation review | Have valuation, sleeve weights, or price-zone readiness materially changed? |
| Fundamental thesis review | Do moat, growth trajectory, competitive position, and governance still support the thesis? |
| Strategic review | Do the China thesis, flip conditions, sleeve targets, hedge policy, and life-stage assumptions still hold? |
| Event-driven review | Does new evidence justify bringing one of the above questions forward? |

The China-overweight flip conditions receive an explicit annual strategic review and an earlier review whenever material evidence demands it.

## 10. Non-Negotiable Invariants

1. Maintain a net-long ownership posture; do not drift into active trading.
2. Valuation and thesis precede technical timing.
3. Routine moves are partial.
4. A `Broken` thesis blocks Buy/Add accumulation.
5. Compounders are not ordinary trading vehicles.
6. The Hedge sleeve is maintained as insurance.
7. Position and sleeve concentration ceilings hold.
8. Human-capital concentration tightens financial exposure.
9. The China-overweight thesis remains falsifiable.
10. A proposed action is not an investor-confirmed transaction and must not be treated as a change in holdings.

## 11. Deliberate Exclusions

- Leverage as a return engine.
- Derivative complexity beyond a simple approved hedge.
- Day trading or minor-trend timing.
- Indicator stacking or unsupported quantitative optimization as the reason for action.
- Unbounded growth-at-any-price reasoning.
- A Buy or Sell conclusion without an explicit valuation or risk basis and thesis judgment.
- Any claim that an unknown position, price, cost basis, capital amount, employer exposure, or transaction is known.
