# Satellite Selection Analysis

> **Status:** transformed chat-project replica; not SOT.
> **Authoritative source(s):** `3_projects/investment-advisor/canonical/templates/satellite-longlist.md`; `3_projects/investment-advisor/canonical/templates/satellite-shortlist.md`; `2_topics/investment-advising/methodology.md`; `3_projects/investment-advisor/canonical/investor-strategy.md`.
> **Conversation boundary:** This document structures candidate-search and finalist discussion. Selection does not establish a holding, transaction, order size, or target price zone.

## 1. Purpose

Selection has two distinct stages:

1. **Candidate search:** establish that the relevant universe was searched broadly enough and apply a cheap first-pass screen.
2. **Finalist selection:** compare the survivors deeply, preserve why alternatives were excluded, and state falsifiable theses.

The sequence is:

```text
theme map -> candidate universe -> coarse screen -> finalist basket -> valuation-gated recommendation
```

Skipping directly from a theme to a preferred name hides search incompleteness and endowment bias.

## 2. Global Search Frame

Before listing candidates, establish:

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Search date | Date of the analysis. |
| Data vintage | As-of date for valuation, growth, and market evidence. |
| Investable universe | Markets, instrument classes, and accessibility constraints. |
| Theme count and position limits | Current Risk-sleeve boundaries from investor strategy. |
| Screening lenses | Named criteria used for Keep / Watch / Drop. |
| Scope exclusions | Career-sector, correlation, mandate, venue, or evidence exclusions with reasons. |
| Known coverage gaps | Areas not yet examined. |

Current market and fundamental claims require sources and dates.

## 3. Theme and Sub-Industry Map

For each theme, discuss:

- theme name;
- macro or strategic anchor;
- relevant upstream and downstream nodes;
- penetration-stage judgment;
- competitive-structure judgment;
- moat-formation mechanism;
- chosen node and rationale;
- deliberately excluded nodes and reasons;
- hidden correlation with other selected themes.

A policy tailwind is not sufficient by itself. The commercial case, competitive structure, and valuation domain must also be examined.

## 4. Candidate Universe

Use one row per considered candidate:

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Name, code, market, and venue. |
| Sub-segment | Candidate's node inside the theme. |
| Coarse valuation | Current valuation domain with source and date; not an entry zone. |
| Growth trajectory | Gaining, stable, deteriorating, or a more precise sourced description. |
| Leadership and structure | Clear leader, contested field, weak structure, or another reasoned judgment. |
| Moat signal | Early evidence, absent evidence, or unresolved evidence. |
| Screen | `Keep`, `Watch`, or `Drop`. |
| Screen reason | One concise reason tied to a stated lens. |
| Revisit condition | Evidence that could change a `Watch` or `Drop` judgment. |

### Coarse-Screen Meanings

- `Keep`: deserves deeper finalist analysis.
- `Watch`: unresolved; specify the missing evidence or condition.
- `Drop`: fails a current criterion; specify whether the failure is structural or potentially reversible.

`Keep` does not mean selected, owned, or recommended.

## 5. Search-Completeness Check

Before moving to finalists, answer:

1. Which relevant sub-segments were considered?
2. Which credible candidates were omitted and why?
3. Did accessibility or evidence quality narrow the universe?
4. Did an existing holding receive an unearned shortcut?
5. Are the surviving candidates concentrated in one hidden factor?
6. What known search gap could materially change the result?

If the known gap is material, state that finalist selection is provisional.

## 6. Finalist Scorecard

For each finalist, provide:

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Innovation and pipeline | Is research and development productive, credible, and commercially relevant? |
| Market-share trajectory | Is the company gaining share rather than merely starting large? |
| Moat evidence | What observable mechanism supports durable advantage? |
| Management and governance | What evidence supports capital allocation, execution, and minority-shareholder alignment? |
| Valuation ceiling | At what growth-adjusted level would the asset cease to be attractive? |
| Thesis | What falsifiable future-leader claim justifies inclusion? |
| Thesis status | `Intact`, `Watch`, or `Broken`. |
| Kill or flip condition | What evidence would invalidate the thesis? |
| Basket role | How is this candidate economically different from the other finalists? |
| Evidence quality | Sources, dates, gaps, and disputed points. |

The valuation ceiling is a do-not-overpay boundary, not a target price zone.

## 7. Held-Name Neutrality

An existing holding is evaluated on the same scorecard as every new candidate:

- ownership is not a positive criterion;
- cost basis is not evidence that the thesis remains valid;
- an existing position that no longer earns its place must be identified explicitly;
- a new candidate does not receive preference merely because its recent price performance is stronger.

This discipline counters both endowment bias and performance chasing.

## 8. Exclusion Rationale

For each serious candidate that does not become a finalist, state:

- candidate;
- source universe;
- exclusion reason;
- criterion that drove the exclusion;
- whether the exclusion is structural or conditional;
- revisit condition, if any.

Preserving the reason for exclusion makes later reconsideration more honest and prevents the same weak candidate from re-entering without new evidence.

## 9. Basket-Level Checks

For the finalist basket:

- confirm that 2–3 names do not express the same underlying bet;
- identify distinct demand drivers or sub-segments;
- check aggregate theme and factor concentration;
- confirm that each name could support an economically meaningful position within the Risk-sleeve limits;
- record A/H venue mix as an output rather than a quota;
- identify unresolved finalist slots and the criterion that will resolve them;
- state the strongest reason the basket could still be wrong.

## 10. Thesis Status Boundary

Keep status vocabularies distinct:

| Stage | Values | Meaning of `Watch` |
|---|---|---|
| Coarse screen | `Keep / Watch / Drop` | Candidate remains unresolved in the first-pass screen. |
| Finalist thesis | `Intact / Watch / Broken` | A selected thesis is under evidence pressure. |

A `Broken` finalist thesis blocks Buy/Add reasoning and points toward reduction, exit, or renewed analysis. Selection analysis itself does not determine that a transaction occurred.

## 11. Conversation Output

Present selection work in this order:

1. Search frame and known gaps.
2. Theme and sub-industry map.
3. Candidate universe and coarse screen.
4. Search-completeness challenge.
5. Finalist scorecards.
6. Exclusion rationale.
7. Basket-level checks.
8. Provisional conclusion and strongest rival.
9. Evidence needed before any valuation-gated recommendation.

## 12. Hard Boundaries

- No finalist that was never considered in the candidate universe.
- No target price zone, order size, or funding source in selection analysis.
- No use of current ownership as a selection advantage.
- No conflation of coarse-screen `Watch` with thesis `Watch`.
- No finalist without a falsifiable thesis and kill condition.
- No recommendation before valuation and portfolio constraints are examined.
- No claim that a selected candidate is already owned or transacted unless the investor supplies that fact.
